The problem

A core charge is your money sitting on someone else's shelf.

Every remanufactured starter, alternator, caliper, injector, turbo, and battery arrives with a refundable charge attached. The shop pays it up front and gets it back only by returning the old unit to the right vendor, in time, and then actually receiving the credit.

Returning a core does not mean the money has been credited.

That gap is where core money quietly disappears. A returned-but-uncredited core is still the shop's money at risk. It stays at risk until the credit is recorded against it, the shortfall is disputed, or someone deliberately writes it off. A box that left the building is not a settled account.

Most one-to-five-bay shops track this on a whiteboard, a clipboard, or nothing at all, because the core module in a full shop-management suite costs more than the problem appears to. CoreCredit does that one narrow job and nothing else.

  • A core that went back last month and was never credited looks exactly like one that was.
  • A credit that comes back short is easy to miss on a statement and hard to prove later.
  • A return window closes quietly, and nobody finds out until the vendor says no.
  • The old unit is in the building somewhere, in a box, and nobody is sure which one.

How it works

Capture, return, reconcile.

CoreCredit follows the way a core actually moves through a shop — and holds it open until the money comes back.

  1. 01 Capture

    Get the charge on the record

    A replacement part arrives with a refundable core charge. Record what it is, who it came from, and what it is worth — before the paperwork goes in a drawer.

    • Amount, part, vendor, and the invoice or repair-order reference — typed, or read from the paperwork with the camera.
    • Assign the old unit to a storage bin so it can be found in the building, usually back in the box the new one came in.
    • Each vendor carries its own return window, so the due date is calculated rather than guessed.

  2. 02 Return

    Send it back before the window closes

    Group everything going back to one vendor into a single return batch, and keep the proof that it left.

    • Local reminders fire ahead of a deadline; overdue cores are called out on the dashboard.
    • A return batch carries its own return reference or receipt.
    • Photos of the part, the box, and the paperwork stay attached to the core they belong to.

  3. 03 Reconcile

    Only a real credit settles it

    The core moves to Returned — awaiting credit and stays counted in money at risk. Returning it does not settle it.

    • Record the credit that actually arrived, against the amount that was expected.
    • A full credit settles the core. A short one moves it to Disputed, with the shortfall shown.
    • A dispute packet can be exported, so the conversation with the vendor starts from the record instead of from memory.

Money at risk

One figure for everything the shop has not been paid back.

Money at risk is the sum of what is still outstanding across every unresolved core. A partially credited core in dispute counts only for what is still short. Returning a core does not reduce the figure — recording a credit does.

Money at risk What each status means for the money
  • Awaiting old core At risk The core charge is on the invoice. The old unit is not in hand yet.
  • Ready to return At risk The old unit is boxed and identified, waiting to go back.
  • Returned — awaiting credit At risk It went back to the vendor. The money has not come back yet.
  • Disputed At risk The credit was short or never appeared, and is still being chased.
  • Credited Settled The vendor credit actually arrived and was recorded.
  • Written off Settled Deliberately abandoned, on the record, as a decision rather than a gap.

CoreCredit cannot make a vendor accept a return or issue a credit. What it can do is make sure a missing or short one never stops being visible.

What is in it

Focused on the money, not on running your shop.

CoreCredit is not a repair-order system, an inventory manager, or an accounting product. It tracks core charges and vendor credits, and it does that thoroughly.

  • Total money at risk

    One figure for everything still outstanding, across every unresolved core. A partially credited disputed item counts only for what is still short.

  • Core items and their history

    Amounts, part details, invoice and repair-order references, dates, status, and notes, with an append-only event timeline behind each record.

  • Vendors and storage bins

    Each vendor carries its own return window. Each bin tells you where the old unit is actually sitting.

  • Barcode and offline OCR entry

    Read barcodes and printed text from an invoice with the camera. Recognition runs entirely on the device — nothing is uploaded for processing.

  • You confirm before anything is saved

    Scanned information always lands on a review screen first. No amount, reference, or date is written to a record until a person checks it.

  • Return batches by vendor

    Group everything going back to one supplier into a single batch, with its own return reference, so a whole run can be reconciled together.

  • Returned-but-awaiting-credit tracking

    The status that matters most has its own place in the ledger, and stays counted in money at risk until a real credit is recorded.

  • Full and short credit reconciliation

    Record the credit you actually received. CoreCredit works out whether it settles the core or leaves a shortfall, and shows the difference.

  • Disputes with evidence

    Attach photos of the part, the box, the invoice, and the receipt, and keep them with the core they belong to.

  • Exportable dispute packets

    Produce a single document for a short or missing credit, so the conversation with the vendor starts from the record instead of from memory.

  • Local deadline reminders

    Notifications are scheduled and delivered by the device itself. There is no push server and no message to anyone else.

  • CSV and JSON exports

    Take the ledger out as a spreadsheet or a portable backup file whenever you want, through the standard share sheet.

  • Works offline. The parts room is not where signal goes to thrive.
  • No account, no sign-in. Open the app and start recording.
  • iPhone-first, with a layout that adapts on iPad.
  • Built for the counter, not for a desk — one-handed, in a hurry, with gloves on.

Local-first by design

Your shop's numbers stay on your shop's phone.

CoreCredit has no account, no sign-in, and no CoreCredit server behind it. There is nowhere for your ledger to go, because nothing was built to receive it.

  • Shop profile, vendors, bins, core records, events, and settings are stored on the device using Apple's on-device storage.
  • No analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, and no tracking of any kind.
  • Barcode and text recognition run offline. Images and recognised text are never sent to Idlery.
  • Photos are attached only when you take or select them, through Apple's own picker.
  • The camera is requested only after you start a scan or a photo capture.
  • Exports leave the app only when you deliberately share or save them.
  • Reminders are local notifications, scheduled and delivered by your device.
  • Subscription purchases are handled entirely by Apple.

Free and Pro

Free to install. Pro when five open cores stop being enough.

The difference between the two is one number: how many cores can be unresolved at the same time. Everything else in the app is the same on both.

  • CoreCredit Free

    The whole ledger, for a shop with a handful of cores open at a time.

    • Track up to five simultaneously unresolved cores.
    • Credited and written-off cores do not count toward the five — settling one frees a slot.
    • Vendors, storage bins, return batches, and credit reconciliation.
    • Barcode and document scanning, evidence photos, reminders, and exports.
    • A sixth unresolved core is where the limit appears.

  • CoreCredit Pro Optional subscription

    The same app with the cap removed, for a shop that carries more than five open cores at once.

    • Unlimited simultaneously unresolved cores.
    • Sold by Apple through the App Store, monthly or annually. Apple shows the price for your storefront at the time of purchase.
    • Manage or cancel it in your Apple Account settings; deleting the app does not cancel a subscription.
    • Restore Purchases brings it back on a new device, because a subscription follows the Apple Account that bought it.

Your records are never held hostage. If Pro lapses, every core you have already entered stays visible, editable, and exportable. The limit applies only to creating a new unresolved core beyond the free five.

Questions

The things shops ask first.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no sign-in, and no password, because there is no CoreCredit server to sign in to. Install the app and start recording cores. It also means Idlery cannot look anything up for you — support can help you use the app, not retrieve records it has never held a copy of.

Where is my data stored?

On your device, in the app's own storage. Your shop profile, vendors, bins, core records, return batches, event history, evidence photos, and settings all live there. They may be included in your ordinary Apple device backups, according to your own Apple settings — that is a backup you make, not a transmission to Idlery.

Why is a core still "at risk" after I returned it?

Because the money has not come back. Returning the old unit moves the core to Returned — awaiting credit, and it keeps counting toward money at risk until a real vendor credit is recorded against it, the shortfall is disputed, or it is deliberately written off.

That is the whole point of the product. A missing or short credit stays visible instead of quietly disappearing the moment the box leaves the building.

Does it work without a signal?

Yes. CoreCredit is local-first and designed to work offline. Barcode reading and text recognition run on the device, and deadline reminders are local notifications that your device schedules and delivers itself.

How accurate is the scanning?

Barcodes read reliably; printed text depends on the invoice. Either way, nothing a scan reads is saved on its own — recognised amounts, part numbers, references, and dates land on a review screen first and become part of a record only when you confirm them. Manual entry is available in every state of the capture screen.

What is free, and what does Pro add?

The free tier tracks up to five simultaneously unresolved cores. Credited and written-off cores do not count toward that limit, so settling one frees a slot. CoreCredit Pro removes the limit. Records you have already entered are never hidden or locked, whatever your entitlement does.

Is this accounting software?

No — and it is not a repair-order system, an inventory manager, a vendor integration, or a complete shop-management suite. It tracks core charges and vendor credits. It has no relationship with your suppliers and cannot make a vendor accept a return or issue a credit.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. CoreCredit exports a CSV of the ledger for a spreadsheet, a JSON backup the app itself can read back, and dispute packets for a short or missing credit. Everything is generated on the device and leaves only through the standard share sheet, when you choose where to send it.
